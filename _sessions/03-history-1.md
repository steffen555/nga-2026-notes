## Early Aros

The church has been a centre for about **1,000 years**. In the early period only **Ribe** was bigger than Aarhus. Settlement at the river mouth from c. **750**; Aros a **trading post** in the **8th century** with harbour, market, workshops and pit houses.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}

**Kong Godfred:** a fortress protected traffic between Jylland and Sjælland. Merchants or robbers — depends how strong you are. Godfred was assassinated, like many other viking kings.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

**Gorm den Gamle** / early **900s** wall and moat; **Harald Blåtand** (**958–987**) expands them. First wooden church west of the ramparts; Kongsvang tithe farm; *leding* harbour; Djursland beacons.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

About **3,000** people lived in Aros; it was strategically important. Aros was a **king city** — there was no fixed capital; the king travelled from city to city. Minting under **Hardeknud** (**1035–42**).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

**948** Reginbrand at German bishops’ meeting; **960** Harald baptised; **965** Aros bishopric mentioned by Otto the Great.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}

**Harald Hardrada** burns the city’s wooden church in **1051** (Aros holds); **1060** Bishop Christian builds the crypt / Skt. Nikolai — called in History 1 the oldest stone building in Scandinavia (more precise: oldest vaulted stone church / oldest room in Aarhus).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %}

**Six runestones** found in Aarhus — expensive, so Aros was very rich.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %} (Trap counts four among the Dominican/Vor Frue foundation stones.){% include cite.html id="ref-trap-aarhus-dominikanerkloster" %}

Aarhus still brands itself as a viking city. The board game **Mølle** appears on a ship drawing from around **1200** — not from that age or from Denmark; much older.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %} **Aros** = river-mouth settlement.{% include cite.html id="2026-10-01-aritra-discovering-aarhus" %}

## Medieval Aarhus c. 1300

The wall was opened to the sea. Denmark is unified, and Aarhus sits in the middle. The **cathedral** has been built **inside** the city. The church is in charge; the king was thrown out and went to **Kalø**.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

**Peder Vognsen** was bishop **1191–1204** and built the cathedral.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %} (Danmarkshistorien gives his episcopate as 1191–1202.){% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}

First town hall in front of the cathedral in the middle of **Store Torv**; the square had the same shape as today.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

Custom noted: if **eight old men** can remember something, then it is law. **Queen Margrethe I** rode the border of **Riis Skov**, separating Aarhus and Vejlby.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

**Jens Iversen Lange** was the biggest loan-giver to the crown; made the church as we know it today; biggest altarpiece in Denmark. Coat of arms: **three roses**.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

**Niels Clausen** **1490–1520** — Reformation begins. Lecture prompt: compare the period to what happened in the rest of the world.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}
