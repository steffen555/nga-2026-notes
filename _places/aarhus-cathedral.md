## Building

Denmark’s longest church (**93 m**), dedicated from the start to **St Clement**, patron of sailors.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}

Work began in the late 1100s (exact year uncertain), driven by Bishop **Peder Vognsen** (from 1191), on a site inside the town ramparts that had held a smaller wooden St Clement church. The Romanesque brick basilica was finished around **1300–1350**.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}

The cathedral marks about **825 years** in **2026**, counting from a traditional start in **1201**. The tour said the building is actually a bit older: **cathedral school** records that mention the church predate that date, so construction must have begun earlier. Roughly a century to build was also noted, though not certain.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %} Published accounts likewise put the start in the late 1100s (uncertain exact year), under Bishop **Peder Vognsen**, then a long Romanesque build and a major Gothic rebuild.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}

Gothic remaking ran c. **1449–1500**. Bishop **Jens Iversen Lange** (1449–1482) raised the nave, vaulted it, built the west tower (his coat of arms), and ordered the altarpiece from **Bernt Notke’s** workshop in Lübeck; Bishop Niels Clausen finished the enlarged choir.{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}

The town gate once lay at Store Torv; the church can be seen from the water.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

## Reformation and fittings

In the old days there were no chairs. At the Reformation some **36–40** side altars were suppressed (day notes: burned); the church went from Catholic to Protestant. Older pictures were painted over and later restored (1900s restorations / fresco recovery).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}

Paintings and memorials show local mayors, businessmen, and nobles. The floor was covered with gravestones; the tour put burials on the order of **3,500** — the floor is unstable and the tally was said to be careless, so treat the figure as approximate.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

**St George and the dragon** fresco dated **1497** in the notes: a female dragon with young — the mother dead, the young one living. Symbolism: you can slay the dragon (remove the problems), but the young will come again later.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}

**Altarpiece** (inaugurated Easter **1479**, Notke): a multi-winged altar with changing faces through the church year; there is a model. The woman in the centre is not Mary alone but Mary’s mother **Anna** with Mary and the Child (flanked by St Clement and John the Baptist). Jens Iversen Lange was the donor.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

It is an **active church**, not only a museum. The main organ (Kastens tradition, later rebuilds) is described as Denmark’s largest (**89** stops on the cathedral’s English page).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}

## Practical (from the tour)

You must live in the parish to marry in the church. Visiting is free; guided tours are paid (voucher). Toilets exist in the church — check whether something is on; Banken food hall is a better option nearby.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

## Ship, leper window, Marselis chapel

**Votive ship *Unity*** (1720): Nordic church-ship tradition; this example is the largest in a Danish church. The cathedral’s own account matches the tour: a Dutch model linked to **Peter the Great**, wrecked off Skagen, bought by Aarhus fishermen and donated — true origin otherwise uncertain.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

**Leprosy / Lazarus window** (northwest): fresco c. **1300**, the oldest surviving art in the cathedral. The tour said the priest could pass **communion** to lepers barred from entering; published cathedral text names it a leprosy window without specifying what was handed out — keep that distinction clear for guests.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}

**Marselis chapel:** bought by the Marselis family in **1702** as a burial chapel with crypt; high-Baroque monument by **Thomas Quellinus**, the largest of its kind in Denmark. The figures are Constantin Marselis’s widow **Elisabeth Carisius** between Constantin Marselis and her second husband, baron Rodsteen.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %} The day notes named **Gabriel Marselis** and sons **Vilhelm** and **Konstantin** under the floor — Gabriel was the family patriarch and Constantin/Vilhelm his sons; the chapel monument itself centres on Constantin’s widow and her two husbands, so treat the tour naming as a prompt to check before guiding.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}
