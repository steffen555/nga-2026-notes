## Building

Denmark’s longest church (**93 m**), dedicated from the start to **St Clement**, patron of sailors — traditionally martyred by drowning with an **anchor**.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-10-01-aritra-discovering-aarhus" %}

Work began in the late 1100s (exact year uncertain), driven by Bishop **Peder Vognsen** (from 1191), on a site inside the town ramparts that had held a smaller wooden St Clement church. The Romanesque brick basilica was finished around **1300–1350**.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %} History 1: Vognsen bishop **1191–1204** and built the cathedral (lex: episcopate **1191–1202**).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}

The cathedral marks about **825 years** in **2026**, counting from a traditional start in **1201**. The tour said the building is actually a bit older: **cathedral school** records that mention the church predate that date, so construction must have begun earlier. Roughly a century to build was also noted, though not certain.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %} Published accounts likewise put the start in the late 1100s (uncertain exact year), under Bishop **Peder Vognsen**, then a long Romanesque build and a major Gothic rebuild.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}

Gothic remaking ran c. **1449–1500**. Bishop **Jens Iversen Lange** (1449–1482) raised the nave, vaulted it, built the west tower (his coat of arms), and ordered the altarpiece from **Bernt Notke’s** workshop in Lübeck; Bishop Niels Clausen finished the enlarged choir.{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %} History 1: Lange was the biggest loan-giver to the crown, made the church as we know it today, biggest altarpiece in Denmark; coat of arms **three roses**.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

**Niels Clausen** (**1490–1520**): History 1 marks him at the start of the Reformation and notes to compare the period with the rest of the world.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

The town gate once lay at Store Torv; the church can be seen from the water.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %} History 1: first town hall in front of the cathedral in the middle of Store Torv; the square had the same shape as today.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %} **Borgporten**: gate to Aros; two towers in the city coat of arms — built after the viking period.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}

## Reformation and fittings

In the old days there were no chairs. At the Reformation some **36–40** side altars were suppressed (day notes: burned); the church went from Catholic to Protestant. Frescoes were covered in white plaster (**1536**) and later uncovered/restored in the **1900s** — the plaster helped preserve them.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-10-01-aritra-discovering-aarhus" %}

Paintings and memorials show local mayors, businessmen, and nobles. The floor was covered with gravestones; the tour put burials on the order of **3,500** — the floor is unstable and the tally was said to be careless, so treat the figure as approximate.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

**St George and the dragon** (**1497**): female dragon with young — mother dead, young one living. Symbolism: you can slay the dragon (remove the problems), but the young will come again later / evil regenerates.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-10-01-aritra-discovering-aarhus" %}

**Altarpiece** (Easter **1479**, Bernt Notke, Lübeck): about **12 m** tall; centre is **Saint Anne Selvtredje** (Anna with Mary and the Child), flanked by St Clement and John the Baptist; apostles on the side panels; top: Coronation of Mary with Gabriel and Michael; gilding c. **23.5** carat. Five folding faces — feast-day, Lent (Passion), Advent (incl. John the Baptist and Clement’s martyrdom). Donor **Jens Iversen Lange** (shown kneeling). Restored **1975–81**.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-10-01-aritra-discovering-aarhus" %}

**Alle Sjæle-billedet** (c. **1500**): three levels — Heaven (Trinity); Earth (Pope Gregory’s Mass); Purgatory (souls aided by angels). Linked to Masses for the dead; paid Masses/indulgences later fed Reformation controversy.{% include cite.html id="2026-10-01-aritra-discovering-aarhus" %}

It is an **active church**, not only a museum. The main organ (Kastens tradition, later rebuilds) is described as Denmark’s largest (**89** stops on the cathedral’s English page).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}

## Practical (from the tour)

You must live in the parish to marry in the church. Visiting is free; guided tours are paid (voucher). Toilets exist in the church — check whether something is on; Banken food hall is a better option nearby.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

## Ship, leper window, Marselis chapel

**Votive ship *Unity*** (1720): Nordic church-ship tradition; this example is the largest in a Danish church. Dutch model linked to **Peter the Great**, wrecked off Skagen, bought by Aarhus fishermen and donated — true origin otherwise uncertain.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

**Leprosy / Lazarus window** (northwest): fresco c. **1300**, the oldest surviving art in the cathedral. The tour said the priest could pass **communion** to lepers barred from entering; published cathedral text names it a leprosy window without specifying what was handed out.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}

**Marselis chapel** (**1702**): burial chapel with crypt; high-Baroque monument by **Thomas Quellinus** (largest of its kind in Denmark) — Constantin Marselis’s widow **Elisabeth (Sophie Elisabeth) Carisius** between Constantin and her second husband, baron Rodsteen.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhus-domkirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-10-01-aritra-discovering-aarhus" %} Dutch merchant **Gabriel Marselis** received Crown lands in Jutland in **1661** (debts of Frederik III); sons **Constantin** and **Vilhelm** settled locally; Havreballegård became **Marselisborg**. Later names: Marselisborg Palace (**1902**), Marselisborg Forests, Constantinsborg, Vilhelmsborg.{% include cite.html id="2026-10-01-aritra-discovering-aarhus" %}
