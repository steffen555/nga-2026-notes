## Sequence on the site

Before the stone church there was a **wooden church** (stave tradition) on this site; it burned during the Norwegian king **Harald Hardrada**’s attack on Aarhus in **1051**.{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhuswiki-vor-frue" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

The **crypt** (c. **1060**), built of **travertine** (*frådsten*) and fieldstone under Bishop Christian, is the oldest vaulted stone church in the Nordic countries and the oldest room in Aarhus — a Trinity church with three aisles and three apses.{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %} Day notes recorded “Travertine” at this stop.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

Around it rose **Skt. Nicolai**, Aarhus’s **first cathedral** (stone church from the decades after 1060 / c. 1080 in parish accounts), outside the town ramparts.{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-vor-frue" %} When the new **Skt. Clemens** cathedral was built inside the walls (from the 1190s), this church lost cathedral status.{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-vor-frue" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %}

## Blackfriars (Dominicans)

**Dominicans** (*sortebrødre* / Blackfriars / preaching friars) took over c. **1230–1240**, rebuilt in brick, and added the monastery wings. Their churches were open to the town.{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-vor-frue" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %} After the Reformation the monks left (Aarhus Dominicans already **1530**); the church became the parish church **Vor Frue**, and the cloister a hospital for the sick and poor.{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-vor-frue" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %}

## Crypt rediscovery and crucifix

The crypt was closed off, used as storage/fuel space, forgotten, and **rediscovered in 1955** during restoration; rededicated as a church in **1957**.{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-danmarkshistorien-vor-frue" %} Day notes said 1956/57 — close, but the find year is 1955.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

The Jesus figure in the crypt is **not the original**: it is a copy of the **Åby crucifix** (c. 1050–1100); the original is at Nationalmuseet. The parish describes a strong, prince-like Christ with viking-period hair fashion — a victorious king over death, which contemporaries might link to Thor.{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %} On the tour it was read as viking-hero style: looking **up**, **braided hair**, **muscular arms** and **big hands**. The notebook also has **Kong Knud**; what that connection was is not remembered — do not guide it until clarified.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}

## Runestones

Rune stones were reused as building stone in the church/monastery. Trap notes **four** runestones among the Dominican foundation stones, including **Århussten 5** (c. year 1000) in the east-gable foundation; several are now at Moesgaard.{% include cite.html id="ref-trap-aarhus-dominikanerkloster" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-steffen-cathedral-latin-quarter" %}
