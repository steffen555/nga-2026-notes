## Early Aros (viking period)

Settlement on the banks at the mouth of Aarhus Å from c. **750** (river ~**13 km** to Skibby).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %} By the **8th century** Aros was a trading post; early on only **Ribe** was larger.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %} From c. **800**: harbour, market, merchants and craft workshops, larger houses and pit houses.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}

**Kong Godfred:** fortress protecting traffic between Jylland and Sjælland; merchants vs robbers depending on strength; assassinated like many viking kings.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

**900s:** Aros as East Jutland traffic hub. Huge rampart and moat from the first half of the 900s (incl. **Gorm den Gamle**); expanded in the second half under **Harald Blåtand** (**958–987**).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %} First **wooden church** west of the ramparts. Royal farm at **Kongsvang** (king’s fields) for collecting tithe/tax. Aros as royal stronghold, war harbour, and gathering place for the *leding* fleet before raids on England. Beacon warning system (*baunehøje*) across Djursland north–south.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}

**948:** Bishop **Reginbrand** of Aros at a bishops’ meeting in Germany — likely planning a bishopric (chronicles: initiative of Hamburg–Bremen).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %} **960:** Harald Blåtand baptised; Denmark Christianised (Jelling stone).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %} **965:** church/bishopric in Aros mentioned by Emperor Otto the Great (again by Adam of Bremen **1070**).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}

**1051:** Norwegian king **Harald Hardrada** burns settlement and the wooden church outside the wall (and some inside); Aros holds and is not taken.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-aarhuswiki-vor-frue" %} **1060:** crypt church by Bishop **Christian** (who took part in the last raid on England); first viking-period stone church **Skt. Nikolai**, used until c. **1240**, forgotten, rediscovered **1955**.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}{% include cite.html id="ref-vor-frue-kirke" %}

About **3,000** inhabitants; **king city** with no fixed capital — king travelled town to town. Minting under **Hardeknud** (**1035–42**).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %} **Six** Aarhus runestones — costly markers of a rich town.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}{% include cite.html id="2026-09-26-louise-vikingebyvandring" %}

Aarhus still brands itself as a viking city. **Mølle** on a c. **1200** ship drawing is much older than that image and not Danish in origin.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

## Medieval Aarhus c. 1300

Wall opened to the sea; Denmark unified; Aarhus in the middle. Cathedral inside the walls; church in charge; king pushed out to **Kalø**.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

First town hall on **Store Torv** in front of the cathedral (square shape as today). Memory-law: if eight old men remember it, it is law. **Margrethe I** rode the **Riis Skov** border between Aarhus and Vejlby.{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}

Late bishops in the cathedral story: **Peder Vognsen** (notes: 1191–1204), **Jens Iversen Lange** (loan-giver to the crown; church “as we know it”; three roses), **Niels Clausen** (1490–1520, Reformation begins — compare to the wider world).{% include cite.html id="2026-09-30-steffen-history-1" %}
