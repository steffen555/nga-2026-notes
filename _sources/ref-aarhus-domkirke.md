## English guide

Source: <https://aarhusdomkirke.dk/english/>

- Building started in the last decades of the 12th century; exact year uncertain. Bishop Peter Vognsen (ordained 1191) drove the project. Romanesque basilica finished about 1350; red brick.
- Gothic rebuilding c. 1449–1500: height, vaults, enlarged chancel with ambulatory.
- Length **93 metres** — longest church in Denmark; dedicated to St Clement (sailors’ patron).
- Frescoes mostly 1470–1520; largest total fresco area in a Danish church. Exception: fresco around the **leprosy window** (northwest), painted c. 1300 — oldest art in the cathedral, only survival from the Romanesque church.
- Font (bronze, Flensburg 1481) donated by Bishop **Jens Iversen Lange**, who had donated the altarpiece two years earlier.
- Altarpiece inaugurated Easter **1479**, workshop of **Bernt Notke** (Lübeck); pentatych. Centre: St Clement, St John the Baptist, and **Mary with her mother St Anna** holding the Infant Jesus. Donor: Jens Iversen Lange.
- Votive ship **“Unity”** (1720): origin uncertain; restorer tradition — Dutch model ordered for Peter the Great, wrecked off Skagen, bought by Aarhus fishermen and donated. Largest votive ship in a Danish church.
- Main organ: begun 1730 by Lambert Daniel Kastens; later rebuilds. Page states **89 stops**, four manuals — largest organ in Denmark.

## Marseliskapellet

Source: <https://aarhusdomkirke.dk/marseliskapellet/>

- Marselis family bought the chapel in **1702** as a burial chapel with crypt.
- High-Baroque monument by **Thomas Quellinus** (1661–1710) — largest of its kind in Denmark.
- Figures: Constantin Marselis’s widow **Elisabeth Carisius** between her two husbands — Constantin Marselis and baron Rodsteen — plus Minerva and Prudentia.
